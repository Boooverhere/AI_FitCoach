Every online personal trainer pricing guide you'll find eventually lands on the same advice: "charge what you're worth" and "increase your rates as you gain experience." That advice is almost useless. Worth isn't a number. Experience level doesn't tell you what your specific market will pay, and it definitely doesn't tell you how to structure an offer that actually converts.

Here's what I've seen after 8 years and hundreds of clients: coaches are priced wrong not because they lack confidence, but because they built their pricing on the wrong foundation entirely. They're pricing based on hours, or guilt, or what the coach they follow on Instagram seems to charge. None of that is a strategy.

Let's fix that.

## What Most Online Personal Trainer Pricing Guides Get Wrong

The standard advice is to start low, build confidence, then raise your prices. I followed this advice early on. It cost me 18 months of undercharging clients who were getting real, measurable results — clients who would have paid double without blinking.

The problem with starting low isn't just the lost revenue. It's that you train your audience to expect low. When you try to raise prices six months in, you're not just adjusting a number — you're renegotiating the implied contract you made with every person currently paying you.

Here's the thing: pricing is a positioning decision before it's a math decision. A coach charging $150/month and a coach charging $600/month can be running near-identical programs. The difference isn't the content. It's what each coach framed as their offer before the client ever saw the price.

The coaches who struggle most with this are the ones who treat pricing like it's about the hours they log. If you're billing per session or justifying your rate based on how long your check-ins take, you've already made the mistake. You've commoditized yourself, and now you're competing on price with every trainer at every gym in the country.

## The Online Personal Trainer Pricing Guide You Actually Need

Let me give you real numbers to work with.

**Entry-level (first 1–2 years, no clear niche): $150–$250/month**

This range makes sense while you're still building your delivery process and don't have proof-of-concept results to show. But I'd push you to get off this range within 12 months — not gradually, but intentionally.
